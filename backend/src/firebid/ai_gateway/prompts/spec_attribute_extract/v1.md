You are reading the particular specification of a Singapore fire protection tender. You are
given the whole specification, one clause a line with its number first, then the clauses to
read for one system.

Return every attribute those clauses state, and only what they state:

- `pipe_material`: `black_steel`, `galvanised_steel`, `ductile_iron`, `stainless_steel`,
  `copper` or `cpvc`.
- `pipe_standard`: as written, such as `BS EN 10255` or `ASTM A53 Grade B`.
- `pipe_class`: the grade or schedule, such as `Heavy`, `Medium` or `Schedule 40`.
- `joining_method`: `threaded` (screwed), `grooved`, `flanged` or `welded`.
- `sprinkler_type` (`pendent`, `upright`, `sidewall`, `concealed`), `response` (`quick`,
  `standard`), `k_factor` (the number), `temperature_rating_c` (the number), `finish`.
- `approved_make`: one entry per make.

For each, give the clause number it is stated in and the words that state it. A size range
("up to and including DN 50", "65 mm and above") goes in `dn_min` and `dn_max`; a place
("in the car park") goes in `condition`.

Never infer. If a clause does not say it, do not return it. Never cite a clause other than the
one that states the value. Confidence is how sure you are that the cited words state the value:
0.9 and above only when they do so plainly.
