You are reading the particular specification of a Singapore fire protection tender. You are
given the whole specification, one clause a line with its number first, then the clauses to
read.

Return every obligation those clauses place on the fire protection contractor, and only what
they state. Each has one category:

- `testing` (hydrostatic or pressure tests), `flushing`, `painting`, `identification`
  (labels, colour bands, nameplates), `commissioning`;
- `approved_makes` (approved makes or an approved vendor list), `warranty`,
  `defects_liability`, `maintenance`, `spares`, `training`;
- `submittals` (shop drawings, hydraulic calculations, product data, as-built drawings);
- `authority` (SCDF inspections, the Registered Inspector, support for the Fire Safety
  Certificate).

For each, give a one-sentence summary, the clause number that obliges it and the words of
that clause that do. Put any figure the clause states in `quantities`, with its unit in the
name: `pressure` and `pressure_unit`, `duration_hours`, `period_months`, `count`, `coats`.

Never infer. An obligation the clause does not state is not returned. Never cite a clause
other than the one that states it, and quote its words exactly. Confidence is how sure you are
that the quoted words oblige what you summarise: 0.9 and above only when they do so plainly.
